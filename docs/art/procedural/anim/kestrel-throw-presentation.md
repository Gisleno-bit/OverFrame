## Evidence and physical constraint

Historical `882cac5` full motion has now been inspected for forward/up/down throws in both facings and back throw in the positive facing. The corrected `d764aaa7` back throw was separately inspected in both facings across all 82 frames; matching implementation `4ddb10315b936f8b70e5957a5adcd986e8039e9c` contact sheets confirm its release. The four current actions primarily distinguish the release arm direction while the supporting stance and hips remain very similar. Forward throw pushes its arm forward, up throw raises one hand, down throw lowers the hand into a forward lean, and back throw opens an arm behind itself while the held victim is still in front. The launches are correctly differentiated; the body's preparation and weight transfer need to communicate the action.

The simulation holds the grabbed victim at the thrower's front (`pos.x + facing * 16`) until release. This is an observed engine constraint, not an art parameter. Do not translate, rotate or teleport the victim into a newly invented pre-release route. Move Kestrel's body around the existing hold, preserve an intelligible hand-to-victim grip, and direct the follow-through along the actual released victim path. Before release, choose a reachable waist/torso surface on the actual opponent instead of aiming at its root or pretending the victim already moved.

## Four distinct actions

- **Forward / braced push:** enter a small hip-and-knee load with staggered support, shoulders drawing back around hands that remain connected to the actual held target. At the real release, drive the chest forward from the rear leg, open the right palm along the outgoing victim, and let the left hand separate toward the ribs. Keep a bent supporting knee rather than rotating the whole straight figure. Retract once as the opponent leaves. The palm's extension is the release, not a second recovery punch.
- **Up / rising lift:** gather with both elbows below the reachable hold and compress visibly through the knees. Preserve the victim's real pre-release position. At release extend knees and torso upward while the right hand leads the actual rising trajectory; the other hand supports at a visibly different height before opening away. The load-to-rise should be visible in the body silhouette and distinguish this from merely raising the utilt arm. No false pre-release lift of the opponent.
- **Down / low press and pop-up:** the actual current down throw launches the victim upward and forward; this is a combo pop-up, not a downward spike. Fold hips and knees into a short low press at the target's reachable lower torso, keep the supporting feet grounded, and rebound out of that compression only after the real release. Do not invent a floor slam, ground-impact flash, bounce or downward victim excursion that the simulation does not contain. Keep its low release and compact rebound distinct from the extended upward lift.
- **Back / cross-body redirect:** keep the actual grip in front during the hold. Preload a torso twist with the right hand guiding the front-side victim and the head preparing to glance along the rearward exit. At the actual release, begin opening the left arm across the body; complete its rearward arc as the real released victim crosses behind Kestrel. The left hand must not finish behind the thrower early while pretending still to grip a victim in front. Let chest and head follow the exit, then unwind once. Keep simulation facing unchanged; this is a local torso/head pose, not an input turnaround or a render-root flip.

The available preparation is whatever inactive hold/throw ticks actually exist. Do not add startup to obtain more anticipation. Freeze the main body action coherently with real hitlag; its pause must not restart the release or advance the victim cosmetically. Scarf and secondary motion remain restrained and noncausal.
